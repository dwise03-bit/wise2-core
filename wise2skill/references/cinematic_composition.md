# Cinematic Composition and Story

## Scene grammar

Compose each scene in layers: a subtle foreground texture or object for depth, a readable subject in the midground, and a scaled environmental background. Light should have a source and purpose—window, practical fixture, sunrise, reflected signage, equipment, candle, or system event—not a generic glow.

## Hero grammar

Name the master scene, the single focus, copy-safe zone, CTA location, visual tension, desktop crop, mobile reframe, and one restrained animation opportunity. Choose lens feel in narrative terms (wide architectural scale, intimate portrait compression, macro material detail), then avoid extreme distortion unless central to the brand.

## Website story

Build a sequence of changing evidence, not a gallery. Typical beats are promise, problem/context, proof of craft or service, product/process, confidence/proof, and action. Select or merge beats based on the business. A commerce site needs product inspection and purchase clarity; a service site needs process and trust; an interactive world may need states and transition maps.

## Transitions

Use shared materials, lighting direction, camera movement, or narrative cause-and-effect to connect scenes. The transition must say where the visitor is going or what changed; do not use a decorative portal merely to appear cinematic.
